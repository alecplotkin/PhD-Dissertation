\begin{singlespace}
ALEC L. PLOTKIN: Optimal Transport--Informed Mapping of CD8 T Cell Fate Across Tissues \\
(Under the direction of Natalie Stanley and Justin Milner)
\end{singlespace}
\end{center}

Single cell sequencing technologies have enabled unprecedented insight into the heterogeneity of cell states, yet their destructive nature means that each cell can only be measured at a single timepoint. Optimal transport (OT) methods have emerged as promising tools for inferring cell trajectories that unfold over the span of multiple timepoints, fitting probabilistic couplings between time-varying distributions of cells. However, interpretation of OT trajectories in the setting of single cell data demands new analytical frameworks. In this thesis, we develop a trajectory kernel mean embedding (TKME) approach for featurizing OT trajectories, creating computational summaries of cell states over time, which can be used for downstream tasks like clustering. We then apply OT to infer trajectories of CD8 T cells during acute infection response, a system that has been particularly difficult for trajectory modeling using alternative methods. We demonstrate that OT recapitulates CD8 T cell population dynamics, and furthermore that TKME smoothly partitions effector and memory cell lineages over time. We extend OT trajectory inference to a novel setting, modeling CD8 T cell migration between distinct biological compartments, and predict that the long-term fates of tissue resident T cells are closely coupled to their times of arrival. Finally, TKME enables us to perform comparative analysis between circulating and resident memory differentiation, revealing context dependent roles for transcription factors TCF1 and AP-4, and underscoring the role of T-bet as a universal regulator of terminal effector differentiation across compartments. Together, this work proposes computational and analytical advances for OT--informed modeling of cell trajectories, with an eye towards facilitating biological discovery in increasingly more intricate experimental systems.
