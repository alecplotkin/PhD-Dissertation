{\normalfont \textbf{ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS}}

I've heard "a Ph.D. is a marathon, not a sprint" more times than I can count---often enough that I've caught myself repeating it under my breath. The truth is, I've always been more of a sprinter than an endurance athlete. For that reason, I'm especially grateful to the people who have supported me along the way and helped me learn how to pace myself.

First, I want to thank my advisors Natalie Stanley and Justin Milner, who have overseen my growth as a scientist over the past five years. I am incredibly fortunate to have had such supportive and thoughtful mentors, who have been enthusiastic about exploring new frontiers of computational immunology. Some of my favorite moments from my Ph.D. have been in meetings with the two of them, drawing a schematic on a whiteboard, plotting out the minutiae of CD8 T cell trajectories. Natalie's expertise in single cell analysis and machine learning has been instrumental for my training, and her guidance has helped me break into the world of computational method development. I truly appreciate how Natalie has made great effort to be available for her students, and I greatly value her feedback and advice. Justin has driven me to reach for ever higher standards in my work, asking me to look beyond the immediate research question for the deeper meaning. While his exacting feedback has helped me hone my research acumen, he has fostered a supportive and fun lab environment, where I have always felt welcome. I am thankful to have two such extraordinary mentors and friends.

I would like to thank my committee members, Tim Elston, Alex Rubinsteyn, Ageliki Tsagaratou, and Caroline Moosm\"uller, for their career guidance and scientific feedback, spanning diverse topics such as mathematical biology, deep learning, systems immunology, molecular biology, computational optimal transport, and the biotech startup scene. Their enthusiasm for my project, and their help in framing it, has been invaluable for me.
